# Aftersales No-Show Risk Flagging — Business Case & AI Use Case

A real, tested Python project pairing a small AI use case with a proper
business case document and executive presentation — built to close a
specific gap for BMW Group's "Intern Strategic Sales, Business
Development & Transformation Projects" posting: the posting explicitly
asks for AI use-case identification, benefit evaluation, decision
documents, and executive presentations, and nothing in my prior
portfolio had produced that combination in a business-strategy format
rather than a purely technical one.

## What this is (read before citing anywhere)

**All data is synthetic**, generated by `src/generate_data.py` — not
real BMW, dealer, or customer data. I have not worked in automotive
aftersales or with a real dealer-management system. The cost-benefit
assumptions (service-slot value, intervention cost, recovery rate) are
stated explicitly as illustrative, not sourced from any real financial
data. What's built here demonstrates the underlying skill — identifying
a plausible AI use case, evaluating it with an honestly-measured model,
and structuring a business case with real sensitivity analysis rather
than a single unchecked number — on a system I could construct and
verify myself.

## What this models

- **`src/generate_data.py`** — synthetic service-appointment generator
  (6,000 records) with a realistic, noisy no-show outcome driven by
  lead time, prior no-show history, reminder status, and other
  directionally-realistic factors plus random noise.
- **`src/model.py`** — a logistic regression no-show risk classifier,
  evaluated with a genuine held-out train/test split (ROC-AUC, and a
  precision/recall-based flagging threshold rather than an arbitrary
  0.5 cutoff).
- **`src/business_case.py`** — a cost-benefit calculation that uses the
  model's **actual measured** precision/recall (not assumed numbers),
  plus a sensitivity table across the one genuinely uncertain
  assumption (intervention recovery rate).
- **`BMW_Aftersales_NoShow_BusinessCase.docx`** — a one-artifact business
  case document: problem, use case, evidence, cost-benefit, sensitivity,
  KPIs, milestones, and an explicit limitations section.
- **`BMW_Aftersales_NoShow_Deck.pptx`** — a 5-slide executive summary of
  the same material, in presentation form.

## Verified against real, measured performance (not just "it runs")

- Held-out ROC-AUC: **0.78** — genuine, useful signal, deliberately not
  near 1.0, since no-show behavior is inherently noisy and a
  near-perfect score would indicate a data-generation artifact rather
  than a defensible model. A test enforces this stays in a realistic
  0.65–0.95 band.
- At a threshold flagging the riskiest ~25% of appointments: 50% recall,
  55% precision — a 2x lift over the 27% base no-show rate. A test
  confirms precision meaningfully beats the base rate, not just "some
  precision."
- The business case's precision/recall inputs are read directly from the
  trained model's held-out evaluation, not hardcoded — a regression test
  guards against these numbers silently drifting from what the model
  actually measures.
- Net benefit stays **positive across the full sensitivity range**
  (15%–50% intervention recovery rate), confirmed by a dedicated test —
  the business case doesn't rest on a single optimistic assumption.

## An honest finding from development

The headline ROI (10.6x) looked large enough to be suspicious on first
calculation, so rather than present it uncritically, I stress-tested it
against the one assumption with no real data behind it — the 35%
intervention recovery rate. Even at a deliberately conservative 15%
recovery rate, net benefit stays clearly positive (€117.5k, 4.0x ROI).
The reason is structural, not a fragile assumption: a €3 reminder call
against a €180 idle service slot is a large asymmetry on its own. This
is disclosed as a sensitivity table in both the business case document
and the deck, rather than presenting only the headline number.

## Running it

```bash
pip install -r requirements.txt
python3 src/generate_data.py           # generate synthetic appointments
python3 -m src.business_case           # train model, compute business case + sensitivity
pytest tests/ -v                        # 7 tests, all verifying real measured behavior
```

## What this doesn't demonstrate

This project doesn't use real BMW, dealer, or aftersales data, doesn't
integrate with any real dealer-management or CRM system, and the cost
assumptions are illustrative placeholders explicitly flagged as needing
replacement with real pilot data. It demonstrates the pattern the
posting asks for — turning a plausible business problem into an
evaluated AI use case, a defensible business case, and an executive-
ready presentation — not claimed automotive-industry or aftersales
domain expertise.
